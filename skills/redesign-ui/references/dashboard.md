# Dashboard redesign guidance

Use this guidance for SaaS dashboards, admin panels, analytics products, CRMs, monitoring tools, device management, billing workspaces, and other operational interfaces.

## Priorities

Treat the dashboard as a workspace, not a marketing page. Optimize for scanning, repeated use, and decision-making.

Prioritize according to the product context:

- current state and items requiring attention;
- primary actions;
- important metrics;
- operational records, tables, or lists;
- trends and history;
- secondary metadata and activity.

Do not default to four equal KPI cards at the top. A metric deserves prominence only when it changes what the user needs to understand or do.

## Structure

Choose the navigation and composition that best fit the task. A left sidebar is optional, not mandatory.

Useful patterns can include compact side navigation, top navigation, a contextual secondary rail, master-detail layouts, a full-width operational table, or an asymmetric overview.

Preserve useful density. A professional dashboard can be dense when the information is structured clearly.

Use tables for structured multi-record data when tables are the clearest representation. Do not convert datasets into cards merely to appear modern.

Keep filters close to the content they affect and distinguish global filters from local module controls.

Place actions near the entities they act on. Avoid putting every action in the page header.

## Status and data

Make important anomalies easier to find than ordinary healthy states. Use restrained status treatment such as text, dots, icons, or subtle tint rather than turning every state into a large pill.

Use charts only when they answer a real question. Do not invent decorative analytics.

Align comparable numbers and data consistently.

## Avoid

Avoid giant headings, excessive empty space, identical card grids, random charts, decorative bento layouts, dozens of colored badges, and landing-page styling inside an operational product.

The result should feel like software a user could operate all day.
