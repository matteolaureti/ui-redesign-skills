# Visual direction exploration

Use this reference when the task asks for a strong visual rethink, several redesign directions, or meaningfully different concepts.

The goal is to expand the **art-direction space**, not only the layout space.

## Separate brand identity from current visual implementation

Preserve genuinely identifying assets such as the product name, logo, and distinctive brand motifs.

Do not assume the current screenshot's palette, typography, background treatment, card style, shadows, radii, chart styling, icon treatment, or surface language are immutable brand constraints unless the user explicitly asks for strict brand fidelity.

A blue product does not require every redesign to remain blue. A light interface does not require every direction to remain light. The source screenshot is evidence of the current implementation, not a mandate to reproduce its visual system.

## Explore different art directions

When multiple concepts are requested, vary both the structural solution and the visual language.

Useful art-direction axes include:

- cool neutral, warm neutral, dark, tinted, or high-contrast surface systems;
- restrained enterprise, editorial, premium, minimal architectural, media-led, brand-forward, or data-first visual character;
- typography personality and hierarchy;
- amount and type of imagery;
- photography, product imagery, content previews, maps, thumbnails, or illustration when product-relevant;
- iconography style;
- data-visualization language;
- border, divider, shadow, and elevation strategy;
- degree of texture, grain, ambient depth, or background treatment;
- density and visual rhythm;
- how strongly brand color is expressed.

Do not mechanically apply a preset list. Select directions that make sense for the product and the user's request.

## Imagery is a first-class design material

Do not avoid imagery by default.

When the product contains meaningful visual entities, consider using high-quality imagery, previews, photography, maps, media, thumbnails, avatars, product shots, document previews, content covers, contextual backgrounds, or other relevant visual assets as part of the interface architecture.

Use imagery where it communicates real product information or creates purposeful visual hierarchy. Do not add decorative stock imagery just to fill space.

A professional dashboard or application does not have to be composed only of cards, icons, charts, tables, and text.

## Diversity requirement

For multiple redesign directions, do not keep every concept inside the same:

- palette;
- typography family;
- surface language;
- imagery strategy;
- icon treatment;
- visual mood;
- chart style;
- brand-color intensity.

Structural diversity alone is not enough. Visual diversity alone is not enough. Strong exploration varies both.

Before finalizing a new direction, compare it with the previous concepts. If it feels like the same art direction applied to a different layout, push the visual language farther while keeping the product coherent and usable.

## Quality boundary

Different does not mean random.

Every direction should still feel appropriate to the product, internally coherent, implementable, and intentionally art-directed. Avoid novelty for its own sake, decorative excess, or visual systems that fight the product's purpose.
